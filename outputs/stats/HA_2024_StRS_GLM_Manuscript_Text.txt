2. DETAILED RESULTS PARAGRAPH:
Hierarchical survey-weighted GLMs revealed distinct spatial architectures across carbonate budget parameters. Live coral cover exhibited a significant latitude-by-region interaction (Panel A; Wald F(1, 323) = 8.42, p = 0.0040), driven by contrasting latitudinal trajectories between the Main Hawaiian Islands (β = -4.81 ± 0.97 %/°N, p < 0.0001) and the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands (β = -0.86 ± 0.95 %/°N, p = 0.3691). In contrast, both gross and net carbonate production were best explained by additive parallel models (Panels B, D), featuring significant shared latitudinal declines across the archipelago (gross production slope: β = -0.82 ± 0.18 kg m⁻² yr⁻¹/°N, p < 0.0001, Wald F(1, 324) = 19.78, p < 0.0001; net production slope: β = -0.80 ± 0.17 kg m⁻² yr⁻¹/°N, p < 0.0001, Wald F(1, 324) = 22.37, p < 0.0001) combined with significant elevated regional baselines in the NWHI (gross production offset: Δ = +4.12 ± 1.26 kg m⁻² yr⁻¹, p = 0.0012, Wald F(1, 324) = 10.71, p = 0.0012; net production offset: Δ = +2.79 ± 1.12 kg m⁻² yr⁻¹, p = 0.0132, Wald F(1, 324) = 6.21, p = 0.0132). Gross bioerosion showed a regional difference only (Panel C; NWHI offset: Δ = +1.33 ± 0.38 kg m⁻² yr⁻¹, p = 0.0005, Wald F(1, 324) = 12.21, p = 0.0005), with no significant latitudinal gradient observed across either sub-region (Wald F(1, 324) = 0.20, p = 0.6545).
